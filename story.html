How it started

Every brand starts with an idea. But rarely is it fully formed from the get-go. Ours is no diffrent.

SYIMI began with a simple desire to build something of my own. I wanted to create something I could not easily find locally, and that desire became the starting point for what would eventually become the brand. I did not have everything figured out from the beginning. SYIMI came together through searching, testing, making mistakes, and learning as I went.

The name came from something I already carried with me. SYIMI is taken from my own name, Musyimi Kitheka, given to me long before there was a business to attach it to. Musyimi means “the person who hunts,” while Kitheka means “forest.” Over time, those meanings became closely connected to the way I see the brand: the hunter represents the search, and the forest represents where that search begins.

Musyimi means the person who hunts.
Kitheka means forest.

The hunter represents the search. He observes, prepares, and knows what he is looking for. His task is to go out, explore what is available, recognise what stands out, and bring home something worth having. That is how I think about SYIMI. I search through ideas, materials, techniques, references, and what already exists in the world around me. Some things lead to ideas I want to develop myself.. Others may already exist in a form I believe is worth bringing into the collection. The responsibility lies in knowing what deserves to come home.

The forest is where that search happens. It is full of possibilities, influences, materials, and things waiting to be discovered. It is also where different elements can come together to form something new. An idea, a material, a shape, a colour, a technique, a detail — each can become part of something greater when the right choices are made. In that sense, the forest is also the creative space behind everything I build and everything I choose to bring forward.

Originality and interpretation

Originality, to me, begins with observation. Every creative field has its own language, shaped by ideas, forms, references, and influences that have come before. In Steal Like an Artist, Austin Kleon explores this idea through the way creative work is built from what we encounter, study, and reinterpret.

I think the important part is what happens after we find something that speaks to us. We look closer. We ask why it works. We decide what deserves to stay and what needs to change. We bring our own experiences, preferences, and choices into the process. That is part of what the hunter means to me: knowing what to look for, what to keep, what to question, and where to take it next.

Inspiration can come from anywhere, and ideas often carry traces of the things we have seen or admired. Two people can arrive at a similar idea through completely different paths. The character of an idea comes from the thought, choices, and perspective that shape it.

What building this has taught me

Building SYIMI has taught me patience. Ideas take time to develop, and they often reveal what they need as I work through them. Every attempt gives me a clearer sense of what works, what needs attention, and where I can do better.

It has also taught me that craftsmanship lives in the details. A proportion gets adjusted. A surface needs another pass. A small detail takes several attempts before it feels right. These decisions may seem small, but they shape how something comes together, how it feels, and how it holds up. Paying attention to them has become part of how I work.

One idea I often return to is kaizen, a Japanese philosophy commonly associated with continuous improvement. The same principle can be seen in traditional Japanese swordmaking. A katana is shaped through repeated cycles of folding, heating, hammering, and refining, with each stage contributing to the final result. The quality comes through the accumulation of many deliberate steps.

SYIMI has developed into this principle. Each experience leaves something for the next one. Feedback brings another perspective, mistakes reveal what needs more attention, and repeated attempts gradually sharpen the work. Curiosity, patience, honesty, and persistence are what keep that process moving.

Still growing

SYIMI is still growing, but the spirit behind it remains the same. I am still searching, still learning, and still paying attention to the details that influence the work. What began as a desire to build something of my own continues to develop with every idea, every mistake, and every lesson that comes with the process.

There is another side to the hunt. The person looking for something to bring home is not always me. It can also be you. You are here searching for something that feels right for your space, something you believe deserves a place in your home. When you find it in SYIMI, the search comes full circle. I have gone looking for something worth bringing home, and you have found something worth taking home.

I do not know exactly where the path will lead. I know that I want to keep building with care, stay open to what I can learn, and remain connected to where SYIMI began. The search continues, and so does the work.

It's the courage to begin with an idea.

The confidence to build from my own roots.
And the determination to keep searching for something better.
