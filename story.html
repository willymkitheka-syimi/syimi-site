How it started

Like many adventures we take, it all started with an idea, and most ideas aren’t fully formed right from the beginning. SYIMI was no different.

My journey with SYIMI began with a simple desire to create something myself. I wanted to make things that I couldn’t easily find around here, and that urge sparked the beginning of what would become this brand. I did not have everything planned out from the start. SYIMI took shape through exploring, trying things out, making mistakes, and learning along the way.

The name itself comes from something personal. SYIMI is part of my name, Musyimi Kitheka, and over time, the meanings of these words became tied to how I see the brand. The hunter represents the search, and the forest is where that search starts.

Musyimi means “the person who hunts.”
Kitheka means “forest.”

The hunter is the one who searches. He observes, prepares, and knows what he’s looking for. His job is to go out, see what’s available, spot what stands out, and bring back something valuable. As the hunter, I look through ideas, materials, methods, influences, and what already exists in the world. Some things lead to ideas I want to develop myself. Others might already exist in a way that I think is worth including in the collection. The real skill is knowing what deserves to be brought into the fold.

The forest is the place where this search happens. It’s full of potential, influences, materials, and things waiting to be discovered. It’s also where different elements can come together to create something new. An idea, a material, a shape, a colour, a technique, a small detail — any of these can become part of something bigger if the right choices are made. In that sense, the forest is also the creative space behind everything I build and everything I decide to move forward with.

Originality and how we interpret things

For me, originality starts with paying attention. Every creative field has its own way of speaking, shaped by the ideas, forms, references, and influences that came before. In his book Steal Like an Artist, Austin Kleon talks about how creative work is built from what we encounter, learn, and then re-imagine.

I think what’s really important is what happens after we find something that resonates with us. We look at it more closely. We try to understand why it works. We decide what to keep and what needs to change. We bring our own experiences, tastes, and decisions into the process. That’s part of what the hunter means to me: knowing what to look for, what to hold onto, what to question, and where to take it next.

Inspiration can strike from anywhere, and ideas often show traces of the things we’ve seen or admired. Two people might come up with a similar idea by taking completely different routes. The unique quality of an idea comes from the thought, the choices, and the viewpoint that shape it.

What building SYIMI has taught me

Building SYIMI has taught me patience. Ideas need time to develop, and they often reveal what they need as I work on them. Every attempt gives me a clearer understanding of what works, what needs attention, and where I can improve.

It has also shown me that craftsmanship is in the details. A proportion might need a slight adjustment. A wooden surface might need another sanding. A small detail might take several tries to get just right. These decisions might seem minor, but they shape how something comes together, how it feels, and how well it lasts. Paying attention to them has become a core part of how I work.

As I create these lamps, one concept I often think about is kaizen, a Japanese philosophy focused on continuous improvement. You see this same idea in traditional Japanese swordmaking. A katana is shaped through repeated cycles of folding, heating, hammering, and refining, with each step contributing to the final result. The quality comes from the accumulation of many deliberate actions.

SYIMI has grown with this principle in mind. Each experience contributes something to the next. Feedback brings a new perspective, mistakes show what needs more focus, and repeated efforts gradually refine the work. Curiosity, patience, honesty, and persistence are what keep this process moving forward.

Still growing

SYIMI is still evolving, but the core spirit behind it remains the same. I’m still searching, still learning, and still paying attention to the details that shape the work. What started as a desire to build something of my own continues to develop with every idea, every mistake, and every lesson learned along the way.

As I finish, I want to introduce the other aspect of the hunt. The person looking for something to bring home isn’t always me. It can also be you. You’re here looking for something that feels right for your space, something you believe deserves a place in your home. When you find it at SYIMI, the search comes full circle. I went looking for something worth bringing home, and you found something worth taking home.

I don’t know exactly where this path will lead. I know I want to keep building with care, stay open to learning, and remain connected to where SYIMI began. We appreciate your support and we hope to serve you the best we can.

It takes courage to start with an idea.
Patience to develop it.
Humility to learn from mistakes.
Discipline to keep improving.
Confidence to build from my own roots.
And determination to keep searching for something better.

— Willy, founder of SYIMI
